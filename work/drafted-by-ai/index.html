Executive summary

AI drafts. People still need judgment.

The design starts with a customer consequence: Marisol, a small-business owner, photographs handwritten job notes and sends an invoice without catching an incorrect amount. The Expert must address both the invoice and her confidence in the feature.

The storyboard connects a live demo huddle, scenario-based practice, an unscaffolded Final Call, a one-page job aid, and two spaced follow-ups. The supplied prototype implements the cold open, slides 1, 6 and 7, and a Results screen. The full learning program remains a design proposal.

Business challenge

Product accuracy and customer trust are different skills.

The scenario asks Experts to explain the boundary between an AI draft and a customer decision, coach a four-point review, diagnose reminder settings, and recognize escalation boundaries. A technically correct answer can still leave the customer feeling dismissed.

Opportunity

Why this was worth solving.

What became possible

  • Explain the draft–review–send mental model in plain language.
  • Coach the review: Who, What, How much, When.
  • Diagnose the cause before selecting a fix.
  • Practice reminder timing and tone within the scenario.
  • Recognize escalation boundaries without making unsupported promises.

Process

See it. Try it. Prove it. Keep it.

01

See it · planned 20-minute huddle

A shared first look, live demonstration, questions and a peer-scored teach-back.

02

Try it · planned 18-minute simulation

Worked examples and scaffolded decisions on one realistic customer call, with Accuracy and Trust tracked separately.

03

Prove it · Final Call design

Remove coaching, introduce a new customer situation, and require at least 6 of 8 points with zero critical errors.

04

Keep it · transfer plan

A mid-call job aid, retrieval lessons on days 3 and 10, and a proposed on-the-job observation checklist.

Strategic design decisions

The decisions behind the experience.

Why open with the mistake?

The cold open establishes customer stakes before feature instruction. The practice then answers the problem learners have already seen.

Why two meters?

Accuracy measures product correctness; Trust measures how the conversation lands. Consequences arrive in the customer’s voice before the coaching note.

Why separate practice from proof?

The Final Call removes scaffolding and uses a different customer situation, so the assessment tests transfer rather than recall of Marisol’s answers.
