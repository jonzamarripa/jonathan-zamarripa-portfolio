Marcus, Live

From choosing the words to finding your own.

What conversational AI makes possible for leadership practice—and what it still has to prove.

Executive perspective

The design question behind this build is practical: how do you help a newly promoted supervisor rehearse a difficult conversation before the stakes are real? The First Sentence explores the consequences of a chosen opening. Marcus, Live extends that idea into a conversation the learner has to sustain.

My strategic proposition is to make purposeful rehearsal easier to access, while keeping the learning objective, evidence and responsibility for evaluation in human hands. Tavus supplies the conversational video layer. The learning design determines whether the interaction is worth having.

This is a working integration and a research-informed hypothesis. The host experience has passed fixture-based tests; live deployment and learner outcomes remain unverified. The research below establishes a rationale for testing, rather than an effectiveness claim for this pilot.

01 · The brief & the challenge

A promotion changes the relationship before it changes the skill.

I chose a frontline accountability conversation because it brings operational pressure and interpersonal judgment into the same moment. Dana is a newly promoted shift lead. Marcus is the experienced colleague who trained her. She needs to address declining performance while remaining open to information she does not yet have.

The fictional brief is specific: Northstar Distribution, DC-04; Shift 01, 06:42 AM. Pick rate is down 30% across three weeks, two trailers are waiting, and the night cutoff was missed by 15 minutes. These numbers establish the scenario; they are not results from a real organization.

The target behavior is to name the performance concern, explain its impact, invite the other person’s perspective and work toward an appropriate next step. It is possible to sound confident and still avoid the issue. It is possible to state the metric and still shut down the conversation. That tension makes this a useful design problem.

Build 01 · The First Sentence

Recognize a consequential choice.

The authored Figma experience presents openings, consequences, a peer debrief and a transfer exercise. It provides a stable narrative and explicit instructional framing.

Build 02 · Marcus, Live

Generate a response and adapt.

The Tavus experience asks learners to form their own words and respond to a conversational partner. It introduces variability while keeping the scenario and learning purpose bounded.

The two builds serve different functions. A novice may need the branching example first; an experienced learner may want direct rehearsal. Access is independent so neither pathway becomes an unnecessary prerequisite.

02 · Why now & what the evidence supports

Skills pressure creates a reason to experiment. Learning science sets the conditions.

The World Economic Forum’s 2025 employer survey projects that 39% of workers’ existing skill sets will be transformed or become outdated by 2030; 63% of surveyed employers identify skills gaps as a major barrier to transformation. These are employer expectations, not a measured rate of skill loss. They strengthen the case for responsive capability development without prescribing a particular technology.[1]

LinkedIn’s 2025 Workplace Learning Report found that 49% of surveyed L&D/HR professionals agreed their executives were concerned about employees having the skills to execute business strategy. It also reported that 71% of L&D professionals were exploring, experimenting with or integrating AI into their work. This is industry context from a learning vendor, not evidence that AI training improves performance.[2]

My interpretation is that L&D needs to become more capable of designing, testing and maintaining practice environments alongside courses, coaching and work experience. This is a strategic direction to investigate, rather than a prediction that every organization will adopt an avatar.

Transfer is a separate design problem.

Blume et al.’s review of 89 studies links transfer with factors including learner motivation and a supportive work environment, and warns that shared-source measurement can inflate apparent relationships. For leadership skills, a convincing simulation must be connected to opportunities to use the behavior, manager support and an assessment beyond the practice itself.[7]

The evidence therefore supports a careful experiment: authentic task → purposeful rehearsal → structured reflection → a new, independently assessed situation. It does not support attaching an efficacy claim to an engaging interface.

03 · Platform choice & trade-offs

Why Tavus was a fit for this question.

Tavus is a conversational AI video platform. Its managed embed brings a real-time PAL—its term for the conversational character—into an existing web page. The documented integration supports responsive video, device checks, captions, conversation controls and lifecycle events. This gives the pilot an interaction layer without requiring a separate video application.[8][9]

I selected it for the next step in this particular learning experience: moving from a filmed character in a fixed narrative to a partner the learner can address in their own words. The face-to-face format is a design hypothesis about social context and rehearsal. Whether it adds value over a voice-only or text-based partner needs comparison.

Fit with the existing build

The supported CDN custom element fits the portfolio’s precompiled React and static-page architecture. Reusing the supplied deployment preserves the existing character, while a page-scoped greeting places the learner in Dana’s role.[8]

Control of the learning boundary

The host page owns the brief, access, reflection and recovery states. Tavus owns device checks and the conversation interface. Magic Canvas stays inside the embed; no duplicate controls or invented tool handlers are added.[8][9]

Alternatives still matter

Authored branching offers reproducible outcomes. Text or voice agents may be sufficient and cheaper to operate. Human roleplay remains valuable for nuanced facilitation. This pilot is a fit-for-purpose selection, not a completed procurement comparison.

Costs and dependencies to examine

A managed service introduces usage cost, latency, provider changes, network requirements and data-processing responsibilities. No cost saving, time saving, accuracy advantage or return on investment has been measured in this build.

04 · From brief to implementation

AI assistance brought design decisions into reviewable code.

Working with Charlie

Using Charlie as my assistant was part of the build process. The practical opportunity was to work from a clearly stated learning brief, examine implementation decisions and refine an experience in ordinary language. I supplied the challenge, audience, scenario and constraints. The documented integration work translated those decisions into the existing portfolio rather than creating a new application.

The record supports a concrete workflow: inspect the repository; read the official embed documentation; build the optional practice; handle lifecycle and failure states; run the existing build; and test the host behavior with controlled fixtures. It does not support an exact count of hours saved or a claim that live conversations and learning outcomes have been validated.

  1. Define the performance situation.

    Keep the operational facts specific, make the learner’s role clear and avoid giving away the character’s private circumstances or an ideal script before practice.

  2. Translate the brief into interaction rules.

    Use the existing deployment. Open only after visitor action. Start the camera off. Preserve the learner’s ability to end. Keep the original text-enabled pathway accessible.

  3. Integrate within the existing system.

    Use Tavus’s official custom element, shared chapter components and approved Industrial Orange identity. Listen for readiness, start, end, mode changes and errors; clean up listeners and the embed when leaving or restarting.

  4. Verify what can actually be observed.

    The build and fixture-based browser checks passed for delayed loading, error handling, reflection, fresh embed instances and mobile layout. A public configuration lookup returned an origin restriction, so account settings and live-call behavior remain a separate verification step.

The ease worth highlighting is architectural: a documented managed embed and AI-assisted implementation made a bounded experiment feasible within the existing site. The strategic work is deciding what deserves that experiment, what evidence would change the decision, and which responsibilities must remain visible as the prototype becomes a service.

Implementation and current status

Official @tavus/embed@latest; deployment 98977e08-afca-4db4-bd21-cb961f9902ce; inline desktop/portrait mobile stage; no automatic conversation creation; no system-prompt replacement; no additional learner context or persistent memory tag supplied. Page configuration disables unique visitor memory and starts the camera off. The greeting is explicitly set to “Hey, Dana. You wanted to talk?” pending inspection of the saved greeting. The deployment memory toggle, allowed origins, retention, media permissions, live greeting and natural endings still require account-side verification. A fresh embed instance is not proof of memory isolation. No learner recording or transcript has been collected by this portfolio implementation.

06 · What I would do next

Test the learning mechanism before expanding the scenario library.

With additional time, I would prioritize evidence and operating readiness over more features. The decision to scale should rest on whether people can perform a comparable conversation later, under different conditions, with an acceptable cost and risk profile.

Assessment is a separate product decision.

A possible rubric could examine the specificity of the concern, clarity of impact, quality of inquiry, response to new information and appropriateness of the next step. It should permit different effective approaches rather than reward a single phrase. Before adding automated feedback, I would compare it with trained human judgments, examine reliability and bias, and preserve a way to question it.

No such assessment integration exists here. The optional reflection is self-assessment. A warm conversation, a completed call, a confidence rating and a transcript each provide different information; none independently demonstrates transfer. Operational indicators such as trailer delays are too multiply determined to attribute to a short simulation without an appropriate study.

07 · Potential beyond Marcus

A reusable practice system starts with distinct performance problems.

These are candidate applications for a Tavus-based experience, subject to scenario design, domain review and validation. They are not additional builds or demonstrated platform outcomes.

Hospitality service recovery

Rehearse acknowledging a guest’s concern, clarifying the issue and proposing an authorized resolution. Ground the scenario in current service policy; test judgment, not whether the simulated guest becomes happy.

Safety escalation

Practice raising a concern across a hierarchy when production pressure is high. Keep emergency procedures fixed and expert-reviewed; the simulation must not improvise safety policy.

Change and adoption conversations

Help managers explore employee objections to a new system, uncover a workflow barrier and agree on support. Connect rehearsal to the actual implementation plan and follow-up.

Coaching and career development

Practice asking useful questions, discussing an aspiration and agreeing on a development action. Keep it developmental; do not infer employee potential or make promotion decisions from simulated conversation scores.

Stakeholder discovery

Rehearse a performance-consulting interview with a sponsor who initially asks for a course. Evaluate whether the learner investigates the problem, work conditions and success measures before recommending training.

Multilingual rehearsal

Explore localized practice with bilingual subject-matter reviewers and culturally appropriate expectations. Test language support, accent accessibility and equivalent task difficulty; this pilot’s original bilingual simulation does not establish that the live version is bilingual.

The future implication for L&D

My thesis is that conversational systems can make repeated interpersonal practice more accessible between facilitated sessions. The design responsibility expands: define credible scenarios, manage variation, decide when an example or a human coach is needed, and maintain the evidence and data controls as models change.

That opens a path toward maintained practice environments organized around work decisions. It also raises a harder question: what makes a digital interaction educationally trustworthy over time? A realistic face and fluent dialogue cannot answer that. A clear task, appropriate challenge, learner agency, feedback that has been checked, and evidence of subsequent performance can help us answer it.

Marcus, Live is my first bounded experiment along that path. Its value as a portfolio piece is the connected reasoning—from an operational challenge, to a learning mechanism, to a technology choice, to an evaluation and maintenance plan. The next milestone is evidence strong enough to decide what deserves to scale.

An inspectable evidence trail.

This is a focused narrative review, not a systematic review or a peer-reviewed white paper. It combines foundational simulation and transfer research, recent AI experiments, industry signals and official implementation documentation. Sources were checked on November 2, 2026. Study populations and interventions differ from this pilot; external results are not presented as its outcomes. Abstract-only access is identified below.

    Employer survey and projections

    Employer expectations for 2025–2030; forecasts are not observed learning outcomes.

    145 empirical studies; overall g = 0.85, 95% CI [0.69, 1.02]. Full article inspected. Broad simulation evidence, not a test of generative AI or this build.

    Peer-reviewed meta-analysis

    Peer-reviewed randomized trial

    Full text inspected. Single center, 56 medical students, four weeks; structured exam outcomes. Different population, task and feedback system from Marcus, Live.
